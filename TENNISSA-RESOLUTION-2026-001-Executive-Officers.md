> ⚠️ **Drafting note for the Board. Remove before signing.**
>
> - This resolution replaces the earlier draft. It appoints three paid officers instead of one, and replaces the compensation tiers based on scholarship funds administered with a fixed $1,000 per month for each officer, starting January 1, 2027.
> - **“Retroactive” is structured as deferred pay, not back pay.** Pay is approved now, before any paid service begins, and accrues month by month from January 2027. It is paid out when operating funds are available. Approving pay after the work has already been done (true retroactive pay) is what the IRS and TENNISSA’s own policies treat as high risk, and this resolution does not do that.
> - **All three officers are Founding Directors.** Each vote is a related arrangement, so approval by the founders alone does not meet the “disinterested director” standard of IRC § 4958 or Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-703. Section 3 requires ratification by independent directors before any payment.
> - **Paid officers are employees.** Wage-and-hour rules, payroll taxes and the tax timing of deferred pay all need confirmation by counsel and a CPA (Sections 2.5 and 4).

## Recitals

**WHEREAS:**

A. Tennessee Islamic School Scholarship Alliance, Inc. (“TENNISSA” or the “Corporation”) is a Tennessee nonprofit corporation organized to advance charitable and educational purposes by supporting eligible students attending eligible Islamic K–12 schools throughout Tennessee, including as a scholarship granting organization under Section 25F of the Internal Revenue Code (“Section 25F”);

B. The Board of Directors governs the Corporation, oversees its finances, establishes policies, and supervises executive leadership;

C. The Corporation needs three executive officers to coordinate operations, compliance and finance as it prepares to accept Section 25F contributions beginning January 1, 2027: an Executive Director, a Compliance Director and an Operational Director;

D. Article VII of the Bylaws authorizes the Board to appoint an Executive Director (§ 7.2), a Compliance Officer (§ 7.4) and an Operations Officer (§ 7.5), and permits a Founding Director to serve as an executive officer. In this resolution, “Compliance Director” and “Operational Director” mean the Compliance Officer and Operations Officer positions under the Bylaws;

E. The Board expects limited operating revenue during 2027, the first year of the Section 25F program. It wishes to set modest, fixed compensation now, before the paid services begin, while allowing payment to be deferred until sufficient operating funds are available; and

F. The Board intends to replace the earlier draft’s compensation tiers, which were based on annual scholarship funds administered, with fixed compensation consistent with the Financial Controls Policy (FIN-001 § 11) and the Conflict of Interest Policy (COI-001 §§ 4 and 11), which prohibit revenue-based or formula-based compensation of insiders.

## 2. Officer Compensation

### 2.1 Rate

Each officer shall receive fixed compensation of **$1,000 per month ($12,000 per year)** for part-time executive services. The total for all three officers is **$36,000 per year**, plus the employer share of payroll taxes.

### 2.2 Start date; earlier service is voluntary

Compensation begins **January 1, 2027**. All services performed before January 1, 2027 are voluntary, uncompensated service. Nothing in this resolution authorizes compensation for any period before that date.

### 2.3 Fixed compensation only

Compensation is a fixed monthly amount. It is not based on, and shall not vary with, contributions received, scholarship funds administered, revenue or any similar measure. No bonus, commission, incentive payment or percentage arrangement is authorized.

### 2.4 Part-time service and time records

Each position is part-time. Each officer shall keep a simple monthly record of hours worked, submitted with the monthly report under Section 9. The Corporation shall comply with applicable wage-and-hour laws, including any minimum-wage requirement. Counsel shall advise the Board on the maximum number of paid hours consistent with the monthly rate.

### 2.5 Accrual and deferred payment (first-year funding)

Because operating revenue in 2027 is expected to be limited, compensation shall be earned and paid as follows:

(a) **Accrual.** Compensation is earned at the end of each month of service beginning January 2027. It accrues whether or not it is paid that month.

(b) **Funding test.** Compensation is paid in a given month only if the Operational Director certifies, and a second authorized person confirms, that unrestricted operating funds are sufficient after providing for: payroll taxes then due; insurance premiums; audit, platform and professional fees then due; government filing fees; and a minimum operating cash balance of $__________ set by the Board.

(c) **Recording.** Earned but unpaid compensation shall be recorded in the Corporation’s books as accrued compensation payable and shown in each monthly financial report.

(d) **Order of payment.** When funds become available, accrued compensation is paid oldest month first, and in equal proportion among the officers owed. No officer may be paid ahead of the others.

(e) **Payment deadline.** Accrued compensation for a calendar year shall be paid no later than **March 15 of the following year**. If funds will not permit full payment by that date, the Board shall decide, on the written advice of its CPA and counsel and before that date, how to treat the remaining balance, taking into account wage-and-hour law and the tax rules for deferred compensation of tax-exempt employers (including IRC §§ 409A and 457(f)).

(f) **Source of funds.** Compensation is paid only from the Operating Fund. It is never paid directly from the Section 25F Scholarship Account. Amounts properly transferred to the Operating Fund as the administrative allocation under FIN-001 § 6.2 are operating funds.

(g) **No interest or security.** Accrued compensation bears no interest, is unsecured, and does not entitle any officer to accelerate payment or direct the use of Corporation funds.

(h) **Voluntary waiver.** An officer may waive compensation for any future month by written notice given before that month begins. A waiver is voluntary and cannot be required as a condition of holding office.

(i) **Separation.** If an officer leaves office, compensation earned through the last day of service remains payable under paragraphs (d) through (f).

### 2.6 No retroactive compensation

This resolution approves compensation **in advance**, before any compensated service begins. It does not authorize payment for any period before January 1, 2027. Any future increase applies only to months beginning after the Board approves it.

### 2.7 Annual review

The Board shall review officer compensation each year, before the start of the fiscal year, using comparability information and the Corporation’s financial position. Any change applies only prospectively.

### 2.8 Expenses

Reasonable, documented business expenses may be reimbursed under FIN-001 § 8. They are separate from compensation. No officer may approve their own reimbursement.

## 3. Approval Process and Conflicts of Interest

3.1 **Separate votes.** The compensation of each officer shall be approved by a separate vote. The officer whose compensation is under consideration shall disclose the interest, provide any requested facts, leave the meeting during deliberation and voting, and not vote.

3.2 **Limits of founder-only approval.** All three officers are Founding Directors, and the three compensation arrangements are related. The Board recognizes that approval by the remaining founders alone may not meet the disinterested-approval standards of IRC § 4958 and Treas. Reg. § 53.4958-6, or of Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-703, which requires approval by at least two qualified directors. Accordingly:

(a) the Board relies on the comparability information summarized in Exhibit A, and on the written advice of counsel or a CPA, that $1,000 per month is reasonable for the duties described;

(b) the Board shall make reasonable efforts to seat at least two independent directors as soon as practicable (which requires amending Bylaws § 4.2 to allow a larger Board). Those independent directors shall review and, if they find it appropriate, **ratify** this compensation;

(c) **no compensation shall be paid** until either (i) at least two independent directors have ratified it, or (ii) counsel advises in writing that payment may proceed. Compensation continues to accrue under Section 2.5 in the meantime; and

(d) each officer shall complete the annual conflict-of-interest statement (COI-001, Appendix A) before compensation is paid.

3.3 **Minutes.** The minutes shall record, for each vote: the interested officer; the disclosure; confirmation that the officer left the meeting and did not vote; the directors present and how they voted; the comparability information relied on; and the reasons for the decision (COI-001 § 15). Minutes shall be prepared before the next Board meeting or within 60 days, whichever is later.

## 4. Employment Status, Payroll and Reporting

4.1 Officers who are paid for services are treated as **employees** for federal tax purposes unless counsel advises otherwise. Compensation shall be paid through payroll, with required income-tax withholding and Social Security and Medicare (FICA) taxes, and reported on Form W-2.

4.2 The Operational Director shall arrange a payroll service. With counsel, the Operational Director shall also confirm the Corporation’s obligations for federal and Tennessee payroll filings, Tennessee unemployment insurance (including any election available to nonprofits), and workers’ compensation coverage.

4.3 Officer compensation shall be reported on the Corporation’s Form 990 as required.

4.4 The employer share of payroll taxes (estimated at 7.65% of wages) shall be included in the operating budget.

## 5. Duties and Authority of the Officers

### 5.1 Executive Director

Subject to the Articles of Incorporation, Bylaws, Board-approved policies, approved budget and applicable law, the Executive Director is authorized to:

- manage ordinary day-to-day operations and supervise the Compliance Director’s and Operational Director’s operational work (but not the Compliance Director’s independent reporting to the Board);
- implement operational plans consistent with the Board-approved mission and strategy;
- coordinate scholarship applications, school participation, donor communications, scholarship administration and student-support processes;
- develop proposed scholarship criteria, procedures, calendars and forms for Board approval;
- maintain relationships with eligible Islamic K–12 schools, donors, professional advisers, service providers, volunteers and community partners;
- coordinate fundraising and donor outreach in accordance with approved policies and charitable-solicitation requirements;
- prepare proposed annual budgets, forecasts, scholarship reports and operational plans;
- appoint, supervise and remove staff or contractors within the authority and budget delegated by the Board; and
- perform other duties delegated in writing by the Board.

### 5.2 Compliance Director

The Compliance Director shall:

- coordinate the Corporation’s Section 25F compliance program under the Section 25F SGO Compliance and Reporting Policy (SGO-001), including the TDOE listing, IRS SGO registration, donor statements, IRS reports and the compliance calendar;
- oversee school reverification, student eligibility verification and disqualified-person screening under the Scholarship Policy (SCH-001);
- serve as Privacy Officer and Records Custodian unless the Board designates others (PRIV-001, REC-001);
- approve credit-related public communications before publication (SGO-001 § 6);
- coordinate the annual conflict-of-interest disclosures and the conflicts register; and
- report compliance status to the Board at every regular meeting, and report material concerns directly to the Board or an independent director.

### 5.3 Operational Director

The Operational Director shall:

- maintain the books and records of the Operating Fund and the Section 25F Scholarship Account under the Financial Controls Policy (FIN-001);
- prepare payments for approval, maintain the 90% scholarship-spending tracker, and arrange monthly bank reconciliations reviewed by a second person;
- administer the SGO platform (currently FACTS Compass), payment processing, payroll, insurance and vendor contracts within delegated authority;
- certify available operating funds for compensation under Section 2.5(b) and maintain the accrued-compensation record (Exhibit B); and
- coordinate the annual audit and Form 990 preparation with the outside CPA.

### 5.4 Limits on all officers

No officer may override Board decisions, amend the Bylaws, change the Corporation’s charitable purposes, approve or influence their own compensation or reimbursement, direct a scholarship award, or exercise powers reserved to the Board.

## 6. Spending and Financial Approval Limits

6.1 **Approved budget.** The Executive Director may authorize ordinary operating expenditures included in the Board-approved budget that serve a legitimate corporate purpose and comply with donor restrictions and applicable law.

6.2 **Routine expenditures.** The Executive Director may approve a budgeted operating expenditure of up to **$2,500 per transaction**. Transactions may not be divided to avoid this limit.

6.3 **Above the routine limit.** Any expenditure, or related series of expenditures, over $2,500 requires approval by the Executive Director **and** the Operational Director, within the approved budget. Any unbudgeted expenditure over $2,500 requires prior Board approval.

6.4 **Unbudgeted expenditures.** An unbudgeted expenditure over **$500** requires prior written approval from the Board or an authorized disinterested delegate.

6.5 **Contracts.** The Executive Director may sign budgeted contracts with a total commitment of up to **$5,000** and a term of up to twelve months. Larger or longer contracts, borrowing, leases and material recurring commitments require prior Board approval.

6.6 **Section 25F funds.** Money in the Section 25F Scholarship Account may be used only for scholarships and the administrative allocation permitted by FIN-001 § 6. It may never be used directly for compensation, fundraising or other operating costs.

6.7 **Dual authorization.** Every payment from the Section 25F Scholarship Account, and every other payment over $1,000, requires two authorized persons (FIN-001 § 4).

6.8 **Payments to officers.** No officer may initiate or approve a payment to themselves. Compensation is paid only through payroll after the funding test in Section 2.5(b). Each payroll run is approved by two authorized persons, neither of whom is the payee for the portion they approve.

6.9 **Prohibited commitments.** No officer may borrow money, guarantee another party’s debt, pledge corporate assets, open a credit facility, or make loans to directors, officers or related parties.

## 8. Scholarship Administration and Independence

The officers shall administer the scholarship program only under the Scholarship Policy (SCH-001) and shall ensure that:

- eligibility and award decisions follow objective, documented criteria and are approved by the Scholarship Committee or another authorized, disinterested body;
- eligible schools are considered consistently under the school-participation requirements;
- no scholarship is awarded to a Section 25F disqualified person, including any family member of a director or officer;
- applications involving any relationship with a director, officer, employee or donor receive independent review under the Conflict of Interest Policy;
- no applicant receives preferential treatment because of a personal, family, financial or business relationship; and
- scholarship funds are tracked and used only for permitted purposes and paid directly to schools.

## 9. Reporting Requirements

9.1 **Monthly financial report.** Within fifteen (15) calendar days after each month-end, the Operational Director, with the Executive Director, shall provide the Board a report showing:

- cash balances and bank reconciliations for each account;
- contributions received, with pledges shown separately;
- Section 25F funds received, awarded and disbursed, and the 90% spending tracker;
- operating revenue and expenses compared with the budget;
- **compensation earned, paid and accrued but unpaid, by officer**, with the hours records under Section 2.4; and
- outstanding liabilities and upcoming commitments.

9.2 **Quarterly operational report.** At each regular quarterly meeting the officers shall report on fundraising, participating schools, scholarship applications and awards, compliance status and deadlines, material risks or complaints, and decisions needed from the Board.

9.3 **Annual report and budget.** Within ninety (90) days after fiscal year-end, the officers shall provide an annual program and financial summary. At least sixty (60) days before each fiscal year, if practicable, they shall submit a proposed budget that includes officer compensation, payroll taxes and any accrued compensation to be paid.

9.4 **Immediate notice.** Any officer shall notify the Board Chair and at least one other director within two (2) business days after discovering suspected fraud, material financial errors, misuse of restricted funds, a significant privacy or security incident, a regulatory inquiry, threatened litigation, or a material conflict of interest. If the matter involves an officer, notice goes under the Whistleblower Policy (WB-001 § 4).

9.5 **Access to records.** The Board and its advisers shall have reasonable access to all corporate books, bank statements, payroll records, contracts and compliance records, subject to privacy requirements.

## 10. Board Oversight and Performance Review

The Board retains ultimate responsibility for governance, financial oversight and supervision of the officers. The Board shall:

- review each officer’s performance at least annually;
- review financial reports, the accrued-compensation balance, scholarship-program performance and compliance status;
- approve the annual budget, scholarship policy, material contracts and significant commitments;
- independently review and document every compensation decision under Section 3;
- require corrective action when needed to protect charitable assets or compliance status; and
- modify or revoke delegated powers, subject to the governing documents and applicable law.

No officer shall take part in deliberation or voting on their own compensation, employment terms, performance-related action, or any transaction in which they have a material personal interest.

## 11. Records, Compliance and Policies

The officers shall maintain corporate, financial, payroll, donor, scholarship and regulatory records under the Records Retention Policy (REC-001). Before accepting Section 25F contributions or disbursing scholarships, the officers shall confirm with the Board and qualified advisers that the formation, tax-exemption, charitable-solicitation and SGO requirements have been met (SGO-001 § 2). The officers shall carry out the Policy and Procedures Manual adopted by the Board.

## 12. Term, Modification and Revocation

This resolution remains in effect until amended, superseded or revoked by the Board. The Board may change any officer’s duties, delegated authority, spending limits, reporting requirements or compensation by later resolution, applied prospectively. The Board may remove any officer under the Bylaws and applicable law. Nothing in this resolution guarantees employment for any term or any future change in compensation. Earned compensation remains payable under Section 2.5.
