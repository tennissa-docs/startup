# Bylaws of TENNISSA

**Tennessee Islamic School Scholarship Alliance, Inc.** · Governing document repository

![Status](https://img.shields.io/badge/status-draft%20pending%20adoption-CC0000) ![Jurisdiction](https://img.shields.io/badge/law-Tennessee%20Nonprofit%20Corporation%20Act-002D65)

This repository holds the Bylaws of TENNISSA, a Tennessee nonprofit corporation organized to become a 501(c)(3) public charity and a **scholarship granting organization (SGO)** under the federal scholarship tax credit (IRC § 25F). The Bylaws set out how TENNISSA is governed: who sits on the Board, how decisions are made, how officers and executive staff are appointed, and the safeguards that protect scholarship funds.

> ⚠️ **Draft. Not yet adopted.** The Bylaws take effect only when the Board of Directors adopts them and the Secretary signs the Certificate of Adoption. **Tennessee nonprofit counsel must review Section 4.4 (Founding Directors) before adoption**, as the Bylaws themselves require. Nothing in this repository is legal advice.

## Founding Directors (Section 4.4)

The current draft makes TENNISSA's three founders **designated directors** with continuing seats:

| Founding Director | Role | Region |
|---|---|---|
| Jimmy Ardiansyah | Executive Director | West/Middle Tennessee |
| Nur Sisworahardjo | Compliance Director | Southeast Tennessee |
| Andi Hermanto | Operational Director | East Tennessee |

**How it works:**

- **No fixed term.** Founding seats are not subject to periodic election.
- **How a seat ends.** A founding seat ends only on (a) voluntary resignation, (b) voluntary written withdrawal, (c) death or legally determined incapacity, or (d) court removal or other action the law requires.
- **Board removal.** The Board cannot vote to remove a Founding Director. Section 4.7 does not apply to them.
- **Amendments.** Any amendment that impairs a founding seat needs that founder's written consent (§§ 4.4, 14.1).
- **After a seat ends.** A vacated founding seat becomes an ordinary seat. There are no successor founders.
- **Equal standing.** Each founder has one vote and the same fiduciary, disclosure and recusal duties as any director. A seat does **not** guarantee an officer or Executive Director role (§ 7.3).
- **Legal basis.** This relies on the Tennessee Nonprofit Corporation Act's treatment of designated directors: term limits do not apply to them (Tenn. Code Ann. § 48-58-105), and designated seats change only through the governing documents (§ 48-58-109). Courts and the Attorney General keep their authority under the Act, including judicial removal (§ 48-58-110).

## Items to resolve before adoption

1. **Counsel review of §4.4.** The Bylaws require it. Ask counsel to confirm the designated-director structure and that the founder-consent clause in §14.1 is enforceable.
2. **Board size versus the website.** §4.2 fixes the Board at **3** directors, but the website shows **5 seats** (two open). To seat independent directors, amend §4.2 (for example, to "not fewer than three nor more than nine, as fixed by Board resolution") at adoption or soon after.
3. **Independent directors.** With three permanent founders, one of whom is the Executive Director, conflict votes can run short of the **two qualified (disinterested) directors** Tennessee requires for conflict transactions (§ 48-58-703). Filling the open seats with independent directors resolves this. See the policy repo, COI-001 §8.
4. **Title alignment.** The Bylaws use *Treasurer* (§6.5) and *Compliance Officer* (§7.4). The website and policy manual use *Financial Controller Director* and *Compliance Director*. Either adopt the titles by Board resolution as equivalents, or align the wording.
5. **Section 25F disqualified persons.** Directors' family members are ineligible for scholarships under federal law. Article VIII §8.6 should be read together with policy SCH-001 §4.6.
6. **Articles of Incorporation.** Confirm the charter does not conflict with designated directors, the "no members" structure, or the dissolution clause required for 501(c)(3) status.

## Adopting the Bylaws

1. Counsel reviews the draft and the tracked-changes file. Resolve the items above.
2. Circulate the final text to all directors at least **7 days** before the meeting (§14.1).
3. The Board adopts by resolution at a meeting with quorum, or by unanimous written consent (§5.7).
4. The Secretary completes the **Certificate of Adoption** at the end of `BYLAWS.md` and the signed `.docx`.
5. Tag the release in Git (e.g. `v1.0-adopted-2026-11-XX`) and keep the signed PDF with the permanent governance records.
6. Include the adopted Bylaws with IRS Form 1023 and the TDOE SGO application.

## Amending the Bylaws

The rules are set by Article XIV:

- **Vote:** two-thirds of directors in office.
- **Notice:** written notice of the proposed text to all directors at least **7 days** before the meeting.
- **Founder consent:** any change affecting §4.4 also needs the **written consent** of each affected Founding Director.
- **Professional advice:** get legal or tax advice before changing faith-based identity, Founding Director governance, executive management, scholarship eligibility or tax-exempt provisions (§14.2).
- **IRS reporting:** report significant governance changes on the next Form 990 (Part VI).

### Git workflow

1. Create a branch such as `amend/4.2-board-size`.
2. Edit `BYLAWS.md` only. Keep section numbering stable. If a section is removed, mark it "[Reserved]" so cross-references don't break.
3. Open a pull request with the proposed text, the reason, and the counsel review note. The PR serves as the 7-day notice draft.
4. **Merge to `main` only after Board adoption.** Record the resolution number and date in the merge commit and tag the release.
5. Regenerate the signed `.docx`/PDF from the adopted text.

`main` always contains the **currently adopted** Bylaws. Proposals live in branches.
